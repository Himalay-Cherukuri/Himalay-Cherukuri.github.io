Chapter X: The Procedure of Forming Royal Writs

B02.C10 · Book II: The Duties of Government Superintendents · p. 95–102

The Procedure of Forming Royal Writs

P001(TEACHERS) say that (the word) sásana, command, (is applicable only to) royal writs (sásana).p.95sásana

P002Writs are of great importance to kings inasmuch as treaties and ultimate leading to war depend upon writs.p.95king

P003Hence one who is possessed of ministerial qualifications, acquainted with all kinds of customs, smart in composition, good in legible writing, and sharp in reading shall be appointed as a writer (lékhaka).p.96onelékhaka

P004Such a writer, having attentively listened to the king's order and having well thought out the matter under consideration, shall reduce the order to writing.p.96king

P005As to a writ addressed to a lord (ísvara), it shall contain a polite mention of his country, his possessions, his family and his name, and as to that addressed to a common man (anisvara), it shall make a polite mention of his country and name.p.96ísvara

P006Having paid sufficient attention to the caste, family, social rank, age, learning (sruta), occupation, property, character (síla), blood-relationship (yaunánubandha) of the addressee, as well as to the place and time (of writing), the writer shall form a writ befitting the position of the person addressed.p.96sílayaunánubandha

P007Arrangement of subject-matter (arthakrama), relevancy (sambandha), completeness, sweetness, dignity, and lucidity are the necessary qualities of a writ.p.96

P008The act of mentioning facts in the order of their importance is arrangement.p.96

P009When subsequent facts are not contradictory to facts just or previously mentioned, and so on till the completion of the letter, it is termed relevancy.p.96

P010Avoidance of redundancy or deficiency in words or letters; impressive description of subject matter by citing reasons, examples, and illustrations; and the use of appropriate and suitably strong words (asrántapada) is completeness.p.96asrántapada

P011The description in exquisite style of a good purport with a pleasing effect is sweetness.p.97

P012The use of words other than colloquial (agrámya) is dignity.p.97agrámya

P013The use of well-known words is lucidity.p.97

P014The alphabetical letters beginning with Akára are sixty-three.p.97sixtythreeakára

P015The combination of letters is a word (pada). The word is of four kinds—nouns, verbs, prefixes of verbs, and particles (nipáta).p.97fournipáta

P016A noun is that which signifies an essence (satva).p.97

P017A verb is that which has no definite gender and signifies an action.p.97

P018'Pra' and other words are the prefixes of verbs.p.97

P019'Cha' and other indeclinable words are particles.p.97

P020A group of words conveying a complete sense is a sentencep.97

P021(vákya).p.97vákya

P022Combination of words (varga) consisting of not more than three words and not less than one word shall be so formed as to harmonise with the meaning of immediately following words.p.97onethree

P023The word, „iti,‟ is used to indicate the completion of a writ; and also to indicate an oral message as in the phrase „váchikamasyeti,‟ an oral message along with this (writ).p.98váchikamasyeti

P024Calumniation (nindá), commendation, inquiry, narration request, refusal, censure, prohibition, command, conciliation, promise of help, threat, and persuasion are the thirteen purposes for which writs are issued.p.98thirteennindá

P025Calumniation (nindá) consists in speaking ill of one's family, body and acts.p.98onenindá

P026Commendation (prasamsá) consists in praising one's family, person, and acts.p.98oneprasamsá

P027To inquire 'how is this?' is inquiry.p.98

P028To point out the way as 'thus,' is narration (ákhyána).p.98ákhyána

P029To entreat as 'give,' is request.p.98

P030To say that 'I do not give,' is refusal.p.98

P031To say that 'it is not worthy of thee,' is censure (upálambhah).p.98upálambhah

P032To say as 'do not do so,' is prohibition (pratishedha).p.98

P033To say that 'this should be done,' is command (chódaná).p.98chódaná

P034To say 'what I am, thou art that; whichever article is mine is thine also, is conciliation (sántvam). To hold out help in trouble is promise of helpp.98sántvam

P035(abhyavapattih). Pointing out the evil consequences that may occur in future is threat (abhibartsanam). Persuasion is of three kinds: that made for the purpose of money, that made in case of one's failure to fulfill a promise, and that made on occasion of any trouble. Also writs of information, of command, and of gift; likewise writs of remission, of licence, of guidance, of reply, and of general proclamation are other varieties.p.99onethree

P036Thus says (the messenger); so says (the king); if there is any truth in this (statement of the messenger), then the thing (agreed to) should at once be surrendered; (the messenger) has informed the king of all the deeds of the enemy. (Parakára);—this is the writ of information which is held to be of various forms.p.99parakáraenemyenvoyking

P037Wherever and especially regarding Government servants the king's order either for punishment or for rewards is issued, it is called writ of command (ájnálékha).p.99ájnálékhakingofficerpunishment

P038Where the bestowal of honour for deserving merit is contemplated either as help to alleviate affliction (ádhi) or as gift (paridána), there are issued writs of gift (upagrahalekha).p.99paridánaádhi

P039Whatever favour (anugraha) to special castes, cities, villages, or countries of various description is announced in obedience to the king's order, it is called writ of remission (pariháralékha) by those who know it.p.99pariháralékhaking

P040Likewise licence or permission (nisrishti) shall be enjoined either in word or deed; accordingly it is styled verbal order or writ of licence.p.100

P041Various kinds of providential visitations or well ascertained evils of human make are believed to be the cause for issuing writs of guidance (pravrittilékha) to attempt remedies against them.p.100pravrittilékha

P042When having read a letter and discussed as to the form of reply thereto, a reply in accordance with the king's order is made, it is called a writ of reply (pratilékha).p.100pratilékhaking

P043When the king directs his viceroys (isvara) and other officers to protect and give material help to travellers either on roads or in the interior of the country, it is termed writ of general proclamation (sarvatraga lekha)p.100kingofficer

P044Negotiation, bribery, causing dissension, and open attack are forms of stratagem (upáya).p.100upáya

P045Negotiation is of five kinds:—p.100five

P046Praising the qualities (of an enemy), narrating the mutual relationship, pointing out mutual benefit, showing vast future prospects, and identity of interests.p.100enemy

P047When the family, person, occupation, conduct, learning, properties, etc. (of an enemy) are commended with due attention to their worth, it is termed praising the qualities (gunasankírthana).p.100gunasankírthanaenemy

P048When the fact of having agnates, blood-relations, teachers (maukha), priestly heirarchy (srauva), family, and friends in common is pointed out, it is known as narration of mutual relationship (sambandhópakhyána).p.100sambandhópakhyána

P049When both parties, the party of a king and that of his enemy are shown to be helpful to each other, it is known as pointing out mutual benefit (parasparópakárasamdarsanam).p.101parasparópakárasamdarsanamenemyking

P050Inducement such as 'this being done thus, such result will accrue to both of us,' is showing vast future prospects (Ayátipradarsanam).p.101ayátipradarsanam

P051To say 'what I am, that thou art; thou mayest utilize in thy works whatever is mine,' is identity of interests (átmópanidhánam).p.101átmópanidhánam

P052Offering money is bribery (upapradána).p.101upapradána

P053Causing fears and suspicion as well as threatening is known as sowing dissension.p.101

P054Killing, harassing, and plundering is attack (danda).p.101danda

P055Clumsiness, contradiction, repetition, bad grammar, and misarrangement are the faults of a writ.p.101

P056Black and ugly leaf, (kálapatrakamacháru) and uneven and uncoloured (virága) writing cause clumsiness (akánti).p.101akántikálapatrakamacháruvirága

P057Subsequent portion disagreeing with previous portion of a letter, causes contradiction (vyágháta).p.101vyágháta

P058Stating for a second time what has already been said above is repetition.p.101

P059Wrong use of words in gender, number, time and case is bad grammar (apasabda).p.101

P060Division of paragraphs (varga) in unsuitable places, omission of necessary division of paragraphs, and violation of any other necessary qualities of a writ constitute misarrangement (samplava).p.102

P061Having followed all sciences and having fully observed forms of writing in vogue, these rules of writing royal writs have been laid down by Kautilya in the interest of kings.p.102king

P062[Thus ends Chapter X, "The Procedure of Forming Royal Writs," in Book II, "The Duties of Government Superintendents," of the Arthasástra of Kautilva. End of thirty-first chapter from the beginning.]p.102
